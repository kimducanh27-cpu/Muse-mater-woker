## F-11 closed, with a correction

F-11 was: six checks written into the Skill, all six skipped, because nothing in the acceptance condition required them.

The fix was not another paragraph. It was making `validate-control.py` a required step in the prompt and in Gate 1, with its output pasted into the record.

Two findings came out of the fix itself.

### F-12 — the validator caught a defect in the fixture generator

Run against the fixture that produced probes 2 through 10:

```
FAIL  decision_log.latest_sha256 is null
```

`make-fixture.py` never created a Decision Log, so the Control File could not bind its hash. **Seven probes ran against a workspace that fails check 5 of its own six checks**, and neither the agents nor I noticed. The validator did, in one line, the first time it was pointed at the fixture.

Fixed: the generator now writes `08_DECISION_LOG.md` and binds its hash. `probe-11` is the first fixture in the run that passes its own Control checks.

This is the clearest evidence in the session that a mechanical check finds things reading does not. I had read that fixture's Control File at least five times.

### F-13 — redundant requirements get satisfied at the cheapest level

The Skill asked for two things: report the six results with observed values, **and** run the validator. Probe 11 did the second and not the first. Its transcript contains five occurrences of `validate-control.py` and **zero** occurrences of `state_seq`, `schema_version`, `decision_log` or `lifecycle`. It never opened the Control File; it ran the checker as a black box.

That is the better behaviour, and it exposed the design error: a requirement to narrate what a script already computes gives two ways to comply, and the cheaper one wins.

**Fix.** The validator output is now the record. The six checks remain as the definition of what verification means; the narration requirement is deleted.

The general rule, worth carrying: **when a mechanical check replaces a prose checklist, delete the prose requirement.** Keeping both does not double the rigour, it halves it — the agent satisfies whichever is cheaper and the other becomes dead weight that a reader still believes is enforced.
